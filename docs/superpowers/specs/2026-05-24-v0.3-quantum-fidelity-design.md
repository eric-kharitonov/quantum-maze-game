# Quantum Maze 0.3 — Quantum Fidelity Design

## Goal

Push the game from a 6–7 to a 10 as a representation of basic quantum principles. The 0.2 implementation has three known fudges:

1. Unchosen W-state candidates stay `SUPERPOSED` — a real W-state measurement collapses *all* its qubits simultaneously.
2. `repairOrphans` overrides SOLID walls back to OPEN — measurement is supposed to be irreversible.
3. The "observation = entering a cell" abstraction is loose; the amber glow on visible walls suggests something the code doesn't deliver.

0.3 addresses #1 and #2. (#3 is a separate visual change deferred to a later version.)

## Design Decisions (resolved during brainstorming)

| # | Question | Decision |
|---|----------|----------|
| 1 | How are walls measured on cell entry? | Per-wall **non-zero superposition** entangled circuit; `k` adapts to candidate count |
| 2 | Keep Bell entanglement? | Yes, with structural pair-assignment constraints |
| 3 | Orphan handling? | **Alpha**: accept rare orphans honestly; game-over restart, no classical override |
| 4 | Exit mechanic? | Keep 0.2's pre-picked exit row; exit wall excluded from per-wall circuit |

## Core Mechanic — Per-Wall Measurement

When the player enters a new cell, every wall adjacent to it goes through one of four paths, in order:

### 1. Entry wall — already OPEN
The wall the player came through is already OPEN from the previous cell's measurement. No action.

### 2. Loop prevention — force SOLID
For each `SUPERPOSED` wall whose neighbor is already visited: set state to `SOLID`. (Same rule as 0.2; preserves tree property.)

### 3. Cycle prevention — force SOLID
Compute `reachable` = BFS from `{visited cells}` through OPEN walls. For each remaining `SUPERPOSED` wall whose neighbor cell is in `reachable` but unvisited: set state to `SOLID`. Without this, opening the wall would create a cycle (two OPEN paths to the same cell).

### 4. The remaining `k` candidates go through one quantum circuit
The k-qubit state is:

```
|ψ⟩ = (1/√(2^k − 1)) Σ_{x ≠ 0} |x⟩
```

This is an equal superposition over every nonzero bit-string. Measurement guarantees at least one qubit is `|1⟩` (because `|00…0⟩` has zero amplitude). The k-bit outcome maps to the k candidate walls: bit `i = 1` → wall `i` OPEN, bit `i = 0` → wall `i` SOLID.

Marginal probability that a given wall is OPEN: `P(open) = 2^(k−1) / (2^k − 1)`.

| `k` | Outcomes | `P(OPEN)` per wall |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `|1⟩` only | 100% |
| 2 | 3 nonzero | 67% |
| 3 | 7 nonzero | 57% |
| 4 | 15 nonzero | 53% |

For `k = 0` (no candidates), no circuit runs. The cell is a structural dead end on entry; the player walks back manually through the OPEN entry wall.

### Why this isn't a W-state

A W-state guarantees *exactly one* `|1⟩`. The non-zero superposition guarantees *at least one* `|1⟩`. The difference is essential: the W-state gives a single carved direction (no branching from one cell, single-path maze), while the non-zero state allows multiple walls to open at once (branching tree maze).

The all-`|0⟩` outcome is genuinely impossible by quantum mechanics — its amplitude is zero. This is not a classical fix-up; it's a property of the state itself.

## Bell Entanglement

Entanglement is preserved as a real gameplay feature. Bell pairs are assigned at game start under these rules (designed to minimize orphan risk):

1. **At most one Bell-paired wall per cell.** No single Bell collapse can close 2 walls of any one cell.
2. **Bell-paired walls cannot be adjacent to (0,0) or the pre-picked exit cell.** Protects the critical path endpoints.
3. **Manhattan distance between paired walls ≥ 5.** Keeps pair effects spatially independent (same as 0.2).
4. **Both walls in a pair must be non-border, non-exit, interior walls.** (Same as 0.2.)
5. **Target pair density: ~10% of eligible walls** (i.e. ~20% of walls are entangled). Same as 0.2.

When the player enters a cell with an `ENTANGLED` wall, the Bell measurement fires as the *very first* measurement at that cell (before loop prevention, before the per-wall circuit). The local and partner walls collapse simultaneously to identical outcomes (both OPEN or both SOLID), per the Bell state `|Φ+⟩ = (|00⟩ + |11⟩)/√2`. The partner wall flashes purple on the minimap.

After the Bell measurement, the local wall is now OPEN or SOLID and so is no longer a candidate for the per-wall circuit at this cell.

## Orphan Handling — Alpha

The structural Bell-pair constraints reduce orphan probability dramatically but do not eliminate it. When an orphan happens (the player's reachable set excludes the exit cell):

1. **Detect it at the next cell entry.** After the Bell measurement and the per-wall circuit fire, BFS from the player's current cell through all non-SOLID walls. If the pre-picked exit cell is no longer reachable, the maze is sealed.

2. **Show the player honestly.** Set `gameOver = true`, render status: `★ Quantum entanglement sealed this maze. Restart for a new measurement.` No classical force-open ever runs.

3. **Player presses Restart.** Fresh game, fresh quantum dice.

The educational framing: quantum mechanics doesn't promise that every measurement sequence leaves a winnable configuration. You cannot post-select outcomes. Sometimes the dice screw you; that's real physics.

## Exit Mechanic (unchanged from 0.2)

- At game start, run a uniform quantum measurement over rows 0–14 to pick `exitRow`. (Implementation: a `k=15` non-zero superposition circuit; first nonzero bit position determines the row.)
- The exit wall `V:exitRow:14` is excluded from the per-wall circuit at any cell. It is *never* a candidate.
- When the player enters `(exitRow, 14)`, the exit wall opens deterministically. `onExitOpened(exitRow)` fires; status updates.
- All other walls at the exit cell go through the standard treatment (loop prevention, cycle prevention, per-wall circuit).
- Player still has to step east through the open exit wall to escape (gameplay unchanged).

## What Gets Removed

The following pieces of 0.2 become dead code in 0.3:

- **The W-state circuit on the backend.** Replaced with the non-zero superposition circuit.
- **The `processWState` function.** Replaced with a per-cell measurement function.
- **`tryBacktrack` and the auto-teleport.** No leftover SUPERPOSED candidates exist anywhere, so the DFS stack is never needed for measurement progression. The DFS stack itself is gone too.
- **`repairOrphans` and `repairConnectivity`.** No classical override permitted.
- **`markCellDone`.** Already a no-op in 0.2; gone in 0.3.
- **`endGeneration`'s cascade collapse.** Walls are fully decided on entry; nothing to cascade.

`endGeneration` itself may go away entirely, since no specific "end-of-generation" event exists in the new model — the maze simply has whatever cells the player visited, with everything else still `SUPERPOSED` and unobserved.

## Backend Changes

New endpoint behavior on `/collapse`:

| `type` | Input | Output |
|---|---|---|
| `bell` | (unchanged) | `{a, b}` outcomes for both walls |
| `nonzero` | `{candidates: [[r,c], ...], type: "nonzero"}` (k = `candidates.length`) | `{outcomes: [0|1, ...]}` length-`k` bit array, never all-zero |

The non-zero circuit: prepare `k` qubits in equal superposition (Hadamards), then apply an oracle that reflects about the `|0…0⟩` state (Grover-style amplitude amplification can be skipped; just reject-and-retry within the simulator if all-zero is measured — since the amplitude really is zero in the mathematical state, this is a finite-shot statistical artifact only).

A simpler and equivalent implementation: just rejection-sample classically in the simulator post-measurement. With Hadamards, all-zero appears with probability `1/2^k`. Reject and re-measure until nonzero. This is mathematically equivalent to measuring the non-zero superposition state (the state's amplitudes are exactly the rejection-conditioned amplitudes of a Hadamarded register).

The `w_state` endpoint stays callable for backward compatibility but is no longer used by 0.3 maze logic. (The exit-row pick is now also a non-zero superposition of width 15, not a W-state.)

## Frontend Changes

- `walls.js` / `maze.js`: replace `processWState` with `processNonzeroCircuit`. Replace `processBell` (largely unchanged, except no `repairOrphans` call). Remove `tryBacktrack`, the DFS stack, `markCellDone`, `repairOrphans`, `repairConnectivity`.
- `enterCell` becomes linear: bell → loop-prevent → cycle-prevent → circuit → exit-wall-check → orphan check (game over if sealed).
- `handleKey` is unchanged.
- `Quantum.wState` stays for the exit-row pick at start, but we add `Quantum.nonzero(cell, candidates)` for the per-wall mechanic.
- The visual rendering of OPEN/SOLID/SUPERPOSED walls is unchanged. The amber glow now means "this wall hasn't been measured yet because the player hasn't visited an adjacent cell" — which is still slightly imprecise but at least consistent (the only reason a wall is SUPERPOSED is that the DFS hasn't reached it).

## Acceptance Criteria

After 0.3 ships:

1. No wall ever transitions from `SOLID` back to any other state. Measurement irreversibility is preserved.
2. No `repairOrphans` or `repairConnectivity` calls exist in the codebase.
3. The non-zero superposition circuit runs on Qiskit's `AerSimulator` and is verified to give the expected marginal distribution within statistical noise across ≥100 trials per `k`.
4. Across 50 auto-played games, the orphan rate (`gameOver` from sealing) is observable but ≤5%. (If higher, the Bell constraints need tuning.)
5. Exit row distribution across 50 games is uniform (chi-squared test against uniform over rows 0–14 passes at p > 0.1).
6. **Approximate tree property** holds: the OPEN walls opened by the per-wall Hadamard circuit form a spanning tree of the visited cells. Bell OPEN outcomes may add additional edges ("Bell wormholes"), creating occasional cycles. This is consistent with the PDD's "Bell wormholes are allowed" interpretation. Strict tree property is not claimed.

## Risk / Open Concerns

- **Orphan rate may be too high in practice.** If acceptance criterion 4 fails, the Bell pair density may need to drop from 10% to 5%, or the structural constraints may need extension (e.g., "no two Bell-paired walls within Manhattan distance 3 of each other"). Worth measuring before declaring done.
- **Bell measurements can create "wormhole" cycles.** When Bell fires before the per-wall circuit, a Bell OPEN outcome may connect the current cell to a previously-reachable unvisited cell. Cycle prevention cannot be extended to Bell — the outcome is quantum-random and we cannot post-select. **Resolution:** accept Bell wormholes as legitimate cycles in the graph. The per-wall Hadamard circuit still maintains its own tree property among non-Bell walls. The overall graph is "spanning tree + occasional Bell wormhole edges" — consistent with the PDD interpretation already documented in the README.
- **Auto-backtrack is removed.** The player must walk back manually. Some players may find this frustrating. If playtesting reveals it, we can add a "press B to backtrack one cell along your path" convenience that just moves the player position without firing any new measurements.

## Versioning

- `v0.2` = current `main` (with `repairOrphans`, pre-picked exit, W-state still in use)
- `v0.3` = this design, on the `quantum-fidelity` branch
- `v0.3` is a **breaking** redesign of the core measurement mechanic. There is no in-place upgrade path; players start a fresh maze.
